## Editorial guards for Batch 2 onwards

### Relative-time language

Static corpus passages avoid unnecessary relative-time wording such as “today”, “this autumn”, “next Thursday” and “this year”. Such wording is permitted only when the genre genuinely requires it and the surrounding correspondence or reporting context makes the reference self-contained. Otherwise, prose uses durable, internally anchored dates or periods.

### Legal jurisdiction and reference clarity

Legal wording must not appear to depend on an unspecified real-world jurisdiction or authority. Rates, statutory processes, deadlines, regulators and similar references are defined within the fictional agreement or context, or use a clearly appropriate real-world reference only where this introduces no unsupported claim. Legal prose remains precise rather than becoming vague for the sake of genericity.

### Structural similarity review

Five-word-shingle Jaccard remains the automated hard gate. A score of 0.00000 is not treated as proof of editorial diversity. Human review additionally checks sentence-opening habits, paragraph cadence, transition mechanisms, contrast structures, final-paragraph devices and authorial voice across genres. No additional numerical hard threshold is imposed without evidence that it is useful.
